## Getting to Panama

Most travelers arrive through Tocumen International Airport, just outside Panama City. Citizens of most countries, including the US, don't need a visa for a tourist stay — just a passport valid at least six months, an onward ticket, and, occasionally checked, proof of at least $500 in funds. Panama uses the US dollar alongside its own Balboa, so there's no currency to exchange and no guessing at conversion rates — what's on the price tag is what you pay.

## From Panama City to Boquete

There's no direct route to Boquete — every path passes through David, the provincial capital of Chiriquí. The fastest way is a short flight from Panama City to David (about 45 minutes), followed by a 50-minute taxi or shuttle up into the mountains. The more budget-conscious route is the overland bus from Albrook terminal to David (roughly 6 hours), then a second, shorter bus or taxi on to Boquete. Driving yourself takes about the same time as the bus, on the Pan-American Highway.

## Why People Choose Boquete

People come to Boquete for a lot of different reasons — coffee, hiking, cooler air after Panama's coastal heat — but underneath all of it is the same pull: this is one of the last places in the country where the cloud forest comes right down to meet you. At 1,200 to 1,800 metres, the temperature drops, clouds catch on the mountainside instead of drifting overhead, and an entire world of orchids, ferns, and rare birds thrives in that mist. It's the reason the resplendent quetzal — one of the most striking birds in the Americas — chooses these slopes and nowhere lower. It's the reason the coffee tastes the way it does. And it's the reason people leave the heat and noise of Panama City behind for a few days of real mountain air.

That's also, simply, what surrounds [**Wild on the Farm, Boquete Cloud Forest Lodge**](/the-farm/). The cloud forest isn't a day trip we send guests out on — it's the ground the cabins are built into, at 1,800 metres, on an organic farm cultivated in the middle of it since 2008. The dry season, January through April, brings the clearest skies and easiest trails; the rest of the year is wetter, but mornings are usually still clear, and the forest is at its greenest.

## Where To Find Real Nature, Silence, and Hiking Away From The Crowds

Most of Boquete's hiking traffic heads toward Volcán Barú National Park — understandably, since it's home to the Quetzal Trail and the summit hike to Panama's highest point. But it's also where the tour buses go, and in high season the popular trailheads can feel more like a queue than a forest.

For something quieter, the better-kept secret is La Amistad International Park, spanning the Panama–Costa Rica border and protecting one of the most biodiverse — and least visited — stretches of forest in Central America. One of its trails is literally named Valle del Silencio: the Valley of Silence. Visitors who make the trip often remark they had the whole park to themselves for the day.
