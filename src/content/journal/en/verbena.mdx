## Medicinal & Therapeutic Properties

Lemon verbena is traditionally regarded as a gentle evening herb, valued for easing everyday nervous tension and supporting restful sleep — one reason it so often appears in after-dinner tisanes. Herbalists commonly describe it as calming to a busy mind while also settling occasional heart-flutter linked to stress, and traditional South American herbal use also draws on it for feelings of anxiousness. On the digestive side, it's traditionally used after heavy meals to ease bloating, cramping, and sluggish digestion, thanks to its soothing, muscle-relaxing character in the gut. Modern research has begun exploring these long-held uses, including a controlled study suggesting regular use may support sleep quality. As with any herbal tea, it's best enjoyed as a supportive, everyday infusion rather than as a treatment for a diagnosed condition — a lovely note to close an evening, not a substitute for medical care.

## Culinary Interest

Fresh or dried, lemon verbena leaves release a bright, clean lemon fragrance without the sharpness of actual citrus juice — softer than lemongrass, less grassy than lemon balm. Its everyday role is the tisane: a small pinch of leaves steeped in hot water makes a fragrant, naturally caffeine-free infusion, which is why it anchors our evening blend alongside jiaogulan and nettle. Beyond tea, cooks use the leaves to perfume simple syrups, custards, and fruit desserts, or to add a citrus note to cakes and jams. The leaf itself is tough and slightly fibrous, so it's typically steeped and removed before serving, much like a bay leaf.

## Where to Find It in Panama

Lemon verbena is native to warm, temperate parts of South America, and it's genuinely hard to find growing anywhere in Panama's tropical lowlands. It thrives at **Wild on the Farm, Boquete Cloud Forest Lodge**, thanks to our cloud forest's unusually cool microclimate at 1,800m, which stays mild year-round — one of the few places in the country able to support it. We harvest young shoots and leaves regularly through the growing season, which encourages bushier growth.
