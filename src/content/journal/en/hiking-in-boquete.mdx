## The Known Trails Around Boquete

### Sendero Los Quetzales

Boquete's most famous trail connects the Bajo Mono side of town to Cerro Punta, on the far side of Volcán Barú. Long before it was a hiking destination, the route was a set of mule tracks used by farmers moving produce between the two highland valleys — which is part of why it still feels like a real crossing, not a loop built for tourists. At around 9–19 km depending on where you start and finish, and reaching 2,500 metres at its highest point, it's named for the resplendent quetzal, most visible here between February and May.

### The Pipeline Trail

Flat, easy, and close to town, this trail follows an old water pipeline — now half-swallowed by moss — from which it takes its name. It's also sometimes called Sendero Cascada Escondida, the Hidden Waterfall Trail, for the thin waterfall waiting at the end inside Volcán Barú National Park. It's popular with birders and one of the most accessible hikes in the area.

### The Lost Waterfalls (Sendero Las Tres Cascadas)

A moderate-to-difficult trail through cloud forest leading to three waterfalls, each a little further and a little wilder than the last. It's one of the most photographed hikes in Boquete, and correspondingly one of the busier ones in dry season.

### Volcán Barú

Panama's only volcano and highest point, at 3,475 metres, was declared a national park on July 12, 1976. It's the single spot in the Americas where, on a clear day, both the Pacific and the Caribbean are visible at once — which is exactly why the summit trail draws the crowds it does, especially for sunrise. Coffee has grown on its lower slopes since the 19th century, brought in part by European settlers whose descendants still farm the region today.

### Hiking Far From The Crowds From Wild on the Farm, Boquete Cloud Forest Lodge

Straddling the Panama–Costa Rica border, La Amistad International Park is one of the largest protected areas in Central America — designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983, extended to include the Panamanian side in 1990. It's also, by a wide margin, the least crowded of the region's parks: remote, only lightly marked, and home to trails with names like Valle del Silencio — the Valley of Silence. ## Hiking From Wild on the Farm

Every trail above has one thing in common: you have to get to it. A drive, a shared shuttle, a parking lot, sometimes a queue at the trailhead — and in peak quetzal season, the most famous routes can feel more like a line than a forest.

At Wild on the Farm, Boquete Cloud Forest Lodge, the trail starts at the door. There's no transfer, no crowd, no other group finishing lunch at the same waterfall as you — just primary cloud forest, moss-wrapped trunks, howler monkeys overhead, and the kind of silence that makes you notice every distant call in the canopy. The farm sits directly at the edge of Amistad International Park, which means the hiking here isn't a curated attraction — it's the same wild, lightly-marked forest that makes Amistad one of the least-visited parks in Central America, except this time the door to it is your own front porch.

If what you came to Boquete for was real nature — not a version of it shared with a busload of other hikers — this is where you'll find it.
